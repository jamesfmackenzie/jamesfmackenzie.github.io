The GameCube's video output story is more complicated than it first appears. The best setup depends heavily on which hardware revision you own, and whether you care about 480p, HDMI, RGB, or CRT-friendly analog output.

*(This post is inspired by [retrorgb.com's GameCube output article](https://www.retrorgb.com/gamecubeoutput.html), reorganized here to flow a bit better.)*

## Two hardware revisions

There are two GameCube models, and the difference matters a lot for video output:

- **DOL-001** — has a "digital out" port.
- **DOL-101** — does not.

If your console is a DOL-001, it unlocks the best output options available, and you should definitely use it.

As a side note: the GameCube actually outputs better image quality than the Wii in general, so if you're being a purist about output quality, stick with the GameCube itself rather than a backward-compatible Wii.

## A note on resolution

GameCube video is often talked about as if it's just 480i, but that's incomplete. A lot of games — especially NTSC titles — also support progressive 480p, and that's the mode you want whenever it's available. Whatever video solution you use needs to handle both 480i and 480p sensibly.

## Best quality: GameCubes with digital out

For a true digital-to-digital setup, use a GCVideo solution like **Carby** or **Prism**.

If you don't have or don't want an HDMI display, the **MK-II** is a good alternative — it exposes a Wii-style output, so you can use a regular Wii component cable with it.

Beyond that, there's a wider range of cable options worth knowing about: official and unofficial component cables, D-Link cables, SCART cables, VGA cables, and so on.

One thing to watch out for: the official digital-out cable doesn't carry audio. You'll need a separate cable into the analog AV port just for sound.

## Best quality: GameCubes without digital out

If your GameCube doesn't have the digital-out port, you're limited to the analog AV port — and here, region matters a lot:

- **PAL GameCubes** are in luck: they support RGB output over the analog port. Pair one with the official SCART cable and you get a picture that's close to what you'd get from digital out. The one downside is no progressive/480p support over this path — so only reach for it if your console lacks the digital port, or your display can't do 480p anyway.
- **NTSC GameCubes** don't get RGB over analog at all. You're stuck with S-Video. :-(

That's a strong argument for keeping PAL and NTSC buying/setup advice separate rather than treating "GameCube video" as one universal answer.
