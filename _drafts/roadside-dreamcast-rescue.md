While out and about in Hong Kong, I stumbled across a roadside stall selling a pile of junk electronics — and sitting in the middle of it was a genuine Sega Dreamcast. A bit of haggling later, and it was mine for **$12**.

At that price, it honestly didn't matter whether it worked or not. It's an **NTSC-J** unit — Japan's region — and despite being pretty grubby, it looked to be in fundamentally decent shape. Worth a rescue attempt.

## First clean

A brief clean took care of most of the surface grime. There were still a few scratches and dings left to deal with, but nothing that looked serious. The real test was opening it up.

Inside, it was remarkably clean — all the capacitors looked healthy, with almost no dust buildup at all. That's always the thing to check first on old console hardware like this, since leaking or bulging caps are usually the actual cause of death, not the case scuffs. And best of all: the power light turned on.

![Inside the Dreamcast after a first clean — capacitors in good shape, minimal dust.](/img/roadside-dreamcast-inside-clean.jpg)

## It works!

Power turning on is one thing — actually displaying a picture is another. A bit of isopropyl alcohol on the video connector's contacts cleared that up, and I was into the Dreamcast's main menu and playing games shortly after.

For $12, this was already a genuine bargain.

![Up and running — the Dreamcast's main menu, working.](/img/roadside-dreamcast-working.jpg)

## Weighing up a shell replacement

The case itself still had several noticeable scratches from wherever this thing spent its life before ending up on that roadside stall. Reproduction Dreamcast shells are readily available on eBay these days, at a pretty reasonable price — including this rather nice clear one I had my eye on.

![A clear reproduction Dreamcast shell, considered as a replacement.](/img/roadside-dreamcast-clear-shell-option.jpg)

## The final result — no replacement needed

In the end, a thorough cleanup — a magic eraser and a decent amount of elbow grease — turned out to be enough. The scratches that looked like they'd need a whole new shell mostly buffed out, and the console came up looking genuinely great.

No replacement case needed after all.

![The finished result — cleaned up, no shell replacement required.](/img/roadside-dreamcast-cleaned-up.jpg)

## The verdict

A $12 roadside find, a healthy set of capacitors, a quick isopropyl fix, and some elbow grease — sometimes that's all it takes. Not every "junk pile" find is going to be this lucky, but it's a good reminder that a grubby exterior doesn't always mean much about what's actually going on inside.
