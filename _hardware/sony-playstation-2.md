The PlayStation 2 is not just an important console to me. It is one I actually lived with properly.

Mine came with me to university, where it ended up at the centre of a lot of multiplayer nights. `Gran Turismo 3` and `Pro Evolution Soccer` got a lot of use, and over time I accumulated far too many accessories, adapters, and VGA gadgets trying to make the PS2 fit whatever screen setup I had at the time.

That long-running relationship is a big part of why I still care about the hardware itself rather than treating the PS2 as something emulators have already replaced.

### What it is

The PlayStation 2 is Sony's hugely successful sixth-generation console. It plays one of the deepest console libraries ever made, and it also has backward compatibility with original PlayStation software.

### Why I keep it

I keep the PS2 around because it still does several things unusually well:

- it has a huge library worth revisiting on original hardware
- it also covers original PlayStation games
- it has a genuinely fun accessory ecosystem
- it is one of those consoles where the display chain changes the experience a lot

That last point matters to me more than it does on many other consoles. The PS2 is a machine I have spent years trying on CRTs, VGA adapters, scalers, and odd video paths.

### My take

My take is that the PlayStation 2 is still one of the best arguments for original hardware.

It is not just about nostalgia. It is about feel, timing, controllers, interlaced video, and the fact that some PS2-era games simply make more sense on the machine they were built for.

I also think the PS2 can look much better than people remember, but usually not by pushing it through random modern flat-panel setups. For me, this is a console where the right display choice matters.

### Pros

- enormous software library
- backward compatibility with the original PlayStation
- great multiplayer and accessory ecosystem
- very rewarding if you care about displays, cables, and video quality

### Cons

- video setup is more complicated than many people expect
- interlaced output can look rough on the wrong display chain
- the console often looks worse on random modern flat panels than it does on a setup chosen carefully

### Other useful things to know

- The software library is deep enough that a single console can justify a lot of time on its own.
- Backward compatibility with the original PlayStation makes it even more useful.
- Video quality depends heavily on what you connect it to. The PS2 can reward careful setup, but it can also look rough if you ignore the signal chain.
- If you like multiplayer, odd accessories, or display experiments, the PS2 keeps getting more interesting rather than less.
