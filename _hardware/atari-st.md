The Atari ST was Atari Corporation's 16-bit successor to its 8-bit home computers, introduced in 1985. It was the first mass-market personal computer to ship with a bitmapped colour graphical interface — Digital Research's GEM desktop, running on Atari's own TOS — at a price well below contemporaries like the early Macintosh.

### What it is

Built around a Motorola 68000 at 8MHz, the ST offered a clean 24-bit address space and performance that held up well against the IBM PC compatibles and Macintoshes of the same era. Sound comes from a Yamaha YM2149 PSG, and — the feature with the longest legacy — every ST shipped with built-in MIDI In/Out ports, which helped make it a dominant platform in music production long after its gaming years had faded.

### The ST family

The line runs from the original 1985 520ST through the [1040STFM]({% link _hardware/atari-st-1040-stfm.md %}) — the model I grew up on — and on to the STE and Mega ST variants. The STFM suffix specifically means an internal floppy drive plus RF/composite TV output, which made those machines an easy fit for a bedroom or living room rather than a desk with a dedicated monitor.

### Why it still matters

- one of the first mainstream computers with a genuine graphical desktop
- built-in MIDI made it a serious music-production platform, not just a games machine
- a large, strong library of arcade conversions alongside real productivity and coding tools
- simple, well-documented hardware that's still straightforward to keep running today
