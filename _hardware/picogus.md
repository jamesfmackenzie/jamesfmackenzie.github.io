PicoGUS appeals to me because it represents the version of the retro-PC hobby I actually want to encourage.

It is not trying to win on rarity, prestige, or collector mythology. It is trying to make old DOS machines easier to use well, which is a very different and much more practical goal.

### What it is

PicoGUS is a modern ISA sound card project for classic DOS PCs. Instead of being original 1990s hardware, it is new hardware built to give old machines practical audio support without the usual rarity and price problems.

### Why I keep it

I like PicoGUS because it solves a real problem:

- original ISA sound hardware is expensive
- good cards are scarce
- many people want usable machines more than museum-perfect ones

PicoGUS shifts the focus back toward building and using old PCs rather than merely hunting parts.

### My take

My take is that PicoGUS is one of the healthiest kinds of retro hardware: modern where it should be, respectful of old systems, and unapologetically practical.

If someone wants a period-correct display piece, that is a different conversation. If they want an old DOS machine that actually gets used, PicoGUS makes a lot of sense.

### Pros

- practical answer to expensive and scarce ISA sound cards
- makes real DOS machines easier to build and use
- focused on utility rather than collector mythology
- good for keeping old hardware accessible

### Cons

- not the right fit if strict period authenticity is the whole goal
- less romantic than using original hardware, if that matters to you

### Other useful things to know

- PicoGUS makes the most sense if you value practical compatibility over strict collector authenticity.
- It is a good example of modern hardware making classic systems more accessible instead of replacing them.
- I find projects like this more exciting than another overpriced original card, because they give the hobby room to grow.
