I already posted a <a href="https://twitter.com/jamesfmackenzie/status/1772633001819590946" target="_blank">thread about these pickups</a>, but there was enough here for a proper blog post too.

Despite some obvious tourist pricing in Tokyo, there were still genuine bargains to be found if you were willing to browse carefully and compare stores. My best finds came from a mix of Akihabara and Shinjuku, and they were a good reminder that Tokyo retro shopping is still fun even when it is not universally cheap.

## Highlights

### Nintendo 64 Bundle

My favourite pickup was easily the Nintendo 64 bundle I found at **BEEP** in Akihabara:

- an unboxed Nintendo 64 with controller for **JPY 6,950** (about **$45**)
- a second matching controller for **JPY 680** (about **$4.50**)

It needed a light clean-up, but the condition was very solid. For a console I had somehow never bought back in the day, it felt like a very satisfying find.

### Wave Race

![Wave Race 64](/img/posts/nintendo-wave-race-64.jpg)

At **Retro Game Camp** in Akihabara, I found a copy of **Wave Race** for **JPY 800** (about **$5.25**) in great condition.

This was exactly the kind of pickup I like making in Japan: inexpensive, iconic, and easy to justify.

### Sega Saturn

![Boxed Sega Saturn](/img/posts/sega-saturn-box.jpg)

At **Surugaya** in Shinjuku, I picked up a boxed **Sega Saturn** for **JPY 14,800** (about **$96**), reduced to **JPY 13,455** (about **$87**) after the tax rebate.

![Sega Saturn and games](/img/posts/sega-saturn.jpg)

I also found some Saturn software surprisingly cheaply, including **Sega Rally** for **JPY 400** (about **$2.60**).

![Sega Rally for Sega Saturn](/img/posts/sega-rally-for-sega-saturn.jpg)

This was one of the better reminders that Shinjuku can still be worth checking, especially when Akihabara starts feeling too polished or too expensive.

### PocketStation

![Sony PocketStation](/img/posts/sony-pocketstation.jpg)

One of the more unusual finds was a **Sony PocketStation** from a bargain bin for **JPY 500** (about **$3.25**).

It was missing the flip cover, but at that price it was hard to complain. These are the kinds of small oddities that make retro shopping in Japan feel more interesting than just hunting for headline items.

### PS2 DVD Remote

![PS2 DVD remote and IR receiver](/img/posts/playstation-2-dvd-remote-and-ir-receiver.jpg)

Another tiny but satisfying pickup was a **PS2 DVD remote and IR receiver** for **JPY 200** (about **$1.30**).

Not exactly a grail item, but definitely the sort of thing that is fun to throw into a basket when you spot it for next to nothing.

## The Broader Takeaway

Tokyo is still brilliant for retro game shopping, but it helps to go in with realistic expectations. Some stores clearly price for tourists, some are better for hardware than software, and some only really become worthwhile when you stumble across a specific bargain.

For me, that was the pattern on this trip:

- **BEEP** was the standout for hardware
- **Retro Game Camp** was good for tidy, reasonably priced mainstream stock
- **Surugaya** was worth checking for boxed consoles and odd surprises

That mix of treasure hunt, price comparison, and sheer variety is still what makes Tokyo such a good place to shop for retro games.
