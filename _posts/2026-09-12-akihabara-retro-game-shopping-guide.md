I already wrote about the specific things I picked up on this trip in [Tokyo Retro Gaming Pickups]({% post_url 2024-03-26-tokyo-retro-gaming-pickups %}). This post is the other half of that trip: a guide to the stores themselves — what each one is actually good for, how the prices compare, and where to watch out for tourist pricing.

## Akihabara

### Super Potato

The famous one — and the pricing shows it. Great selection, but firmly tourist prices. Spread across multiple small floors, with some genuine "holy grail" finds along the way, including an NES/SNES switcher unit, and an arcade upstairs.

Here's the concrete version of "tourist prices". This is a loose Nintendo 64 with a yellow controller, with a sticker grading the 3D stick as 良好 — "good" — at **¥14,850**:

![A loose Nintendo 64 and yellow controller at Super Potato, priced at ¥14,850](/img/akihabara-super-potato-nintendo-64-price.jpg){: width="480"}

A few hours later I bought a comparable N64 and controller at BEEP for **¥6,950**. Same console, less than half the price, about a ten-minute walk away.

The Saturns told the same story. This one is loose, unboxed, and has yellowed badly — ¥11,880:

![A heavily yellowed loose Sega Saturn at Super Potato, priced at ¥11,880](/img/akihabara-super-potato-sega-saturn-yellowed.jpg){: width="480"}

For not much more than that, I later picked up a *boxed* one in Shinjuku.

### Book Off

Less a dedicated games shop, more a large secondhand store that happens to carry a huge number of games. The ground floor is packed with used PS2, PS3, Vita, and Wii titles sitting alongside modern releases — genuinely enormous stock, and prices here beat Super Potato. The retro-specific side is thinner, though: N64 is very small, SNES only a little better, and hardware is close to nonexistent beyond a few handhelds. The upper floors make up for it with a huge amount of anime and manga.

### Retro Game Camp

A small, two-floor store, clearly well organized and well cared for. Prices land between Super Potato and the cheaper end of the scale (Surugaya, BEEP). Strong on the "mainstream" consoles — plenty of N64, Famicom, PlayStation, and Saturn, all bagged and ready to go — and the software pricing is where it really shines. The two Super Mario 64 carts at the top of this post were ¥1,880 and ¥2,080, and I picked up Wave Race here for ¥800.

Hardware is less consistently a bargain. This boxed Saturn was ¥19,800 — the most expensive of the three I looked at on the trip:

![A boxed Sega Saturn at Retro Game Camp, priced at ¥19,800](/img/akihabara-retro-game-camp-boxed-saturn.jpg){: width="480"}

### BEEP

A small basement store, but the best of the bunch for arcade gear and hardware. This is where I picked up my Nintendo 64 — ¥6,950 for the console and a controller, plus ¥680 for a second one.

BEEP is also home to genuine "holy grail" PC hardware — MSX, PC-88, PC-98, X68000 — the kind of thing that's genuinely hard to find outside a store like this. Everything is tagged with the store's own condition card, which spells out whether the item has been tested, and what the warranty is. This is a Mitsubishi MULTI8, at ¥34,800:

![A Mitsubishi Electric MULTI8 home computer at BEEP, priced at ¥34,800](/img/akihabara-beep-mitsubishi-multi8.jpg)

### Surugaya (Akihabara branch)

The video game section here is tiny, tucked into the basement. What it lacks in size it makes up for in quality — some really nice specialty items, mint boxed copies of rare and limited-edition games, and good prices. Not the place to go if you want mainstream retro consoles or hardware in volume, though.

## Shinjuku

### Surugaya (Shinjuku branch)

A much bigger branch, on the 5th floor of a shopping mall, with a noticeably larger video game section than the Akihabara store. Prices are good — maybe marginally higher than Akihabara — but the extra selection more than makes up for it. This is where I picked up the boxed Saturn from the [pickups post]({% post_url 2024-03-26-tokyo-retro-gaming-pickups %}), for ¥14,800 — ¥13,455 after the tax rebate.

Their N64s were keenly priced too. This one came with the Expansion Pak for ¥8,500, with the original ¥15,400 retail price printed on the tag for comparison:

![A used Nintendo 64 with Expansion Pak at Surugaya Shinjuku, priced at ¥8,500](/img/shinjuku-surugaya-nintendo-64-expansion-pak.jpg){: width="480"}

## The same console, three prices

Worth laying out side by side, because this is the entire argument for shopping around:

| Item | Store | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Nintendo 64 + controller | Super Potato | ¥14,850 |
| Nintendo 64 + Expansion Pak | Surugaya (Shinjuku) | ¥8,500 |
| Nintendo 64 + controller | BEEP | **¥6,950** |
| Sega Saturn, loose, yellowed | Super Potato | ¥11,880 |
| Sega Saturn, boxed | Retro Game Camp | ¥19,800 |
| Sega Saturn, boxed | Surugaya (Shinjuku) | **¥14,800** |

All within a few days of each other, and — for the Akihabara stores — within about a ten-minute walk.

## One more stop: Tsukumo

Worth a mention even though it's more PC-parts than games: **Tsukumo PC Main Store, Reuse Hall**, the only secondhand specialty store in Tsukumo, Akihabara. The first floor has a wide range of used PCs and monitors — everything from business machines with office software to gaming rigs — plus secondhand iPhones. The second floor is junk/parts territory: used CPUs, motherboards, memory, and graphics cards.

## The takeaway

The sheer selection across these stores is genuinely amazing — worth the trip just to browse. But prices vary wildly from store to store, and it pays to know that going in. Super Potato is the clearest example of tourist pricing; Book Off, Retro Game Camp, BEEP, and Surugaya are all, in their own way, better value if you know what you're looking for.
